1. Platforms: what is needed and where

PixelPad is two programs that talk to each other, plus the places they are built, shipped and updated from.

2. The system

Everything the phone does goes to the PC as small sealed packets. The PC replies with a few of its own: pongs, rumble and profiles.

3. The wire: packets and sealed frames

Every message is a 16-byte packet whose first byte is the mode. With a pairing key, each packet travels inside a sealed frame: signed and encrypted, new for every packet.

ModeDirectionMeaning
0app to PCTrackpad: action, raw movement, clicks
1app to PCPen: action, buttons, position, pressure, tilt, and the time since the previous sample
2app to PCController: button bits, two sticks, two triggers
3bothPing and pong: round trip time, colour, player slot, the PC's screen size
4app to PCHello: the size of the drawing area
6app to PCKey: virtual key, modifiers, tap, press or release
7app to PCConfig: pen cursor ring, smoothing delay
8app to PCTouch: one finger of a multi-finger gesture
9bothShortcut recording from the PC keyboard
10bothProfiles: list, get, save, delete, and the "a linked game started" notice. Text travels in 11-byte chunks.
11PC to appRumble: heavy and light motor

The nonce changes every time PixelPad Desk starts, so a recording of an earlier run is worthless. The counter makes each frame usable once (the PC keeps a 64-frame replay window per session). Replies are bound to the session they answer. A "discovery" ping sealed with nonce 0 is the only thing the PC accepts without knowing the nonce, and it changes nothing.
